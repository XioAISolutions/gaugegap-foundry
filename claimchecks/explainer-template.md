# Foundry explainer template (stolen from the reel, fixed by the Foundry)

@ffppod's format works: hook, plain-language mechanism, why it matters. The Foundry
adds the one thing the reel skips: the claim boundary. Use this skeleton for any
track, forge, or instrument you want a non-physicist to understand.

## 1. Hook (one surprising sentence)
Say the most interesting true thing. Check it before you write it.

## 2. Mechanism in plain words (one analogy max)
How it works, no jargon. If you need a second analogy, the first one failed.

## 3. Why it matters (one real use)
A concrete thing this enables, not "revolutionizing everything."

## 4. The claim boundary (the Foundry addition)
What this does NOT prove. What would kill the claim. Where the statement stops
being true. One or two sentences, explicit.

## Example (AFM)
1. An AFM feels a surface the way a fingertip reads Braille — but a billion times smaller.
2. A tiny diving board (cantilever) with a sharp tip drags across the sample; a laser
   watches the board bend, and the bending draws the map.
3. It images things light can't resolve: DNA, crystal lattices, semiconductor defects.
4. Boundary: the tip is 5–10 nm wide (tens of atoms), not "thinner than an atom."
   Atomic-scale imaging needs special tips and vacuum conditions.
