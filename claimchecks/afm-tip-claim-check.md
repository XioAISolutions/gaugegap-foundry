# Claim check: "an AFM tip thinner than an atom"

**Source:** @ffppod ("From First Principles") Instagram reel, Oct 5, 2026 — an Atomic Force Microscope explainer.

## The claim

> "Imagine scanning a surface with a tip thinner than an atom!"

## The check

A standard AFM probe is microfabricated from silicon or silicon nitride. Five independent sources put its tip radius at **5–10 nm**:

- Springer, *Nanomanufacturing and Metrology* (2022): nominal tip radius **8 nm** for standard silicon probes
- NCI Nanotechnology Characterization Lab: "tip radius of curvature on the order of nanometers"
- Univ. of Washington SFM lab manual: tip radius **~10 nm**
- UIUC PHYS 403 AFM lecture (2025): common tip radius of curvature **< 10 nm**
- Rubicon Science overview: tip radius **5–10 nm**

An atom is about **0.1–0.3 nm** across. The tip is therefore roughly **20–100x wider than an atom** — tens of atoms wide, not thinner than one.

## Verdict: FALSIFIED

The hook is wrong for a standard AFM. The corrected claim: *the tip is a few nanometers wide (tens of atoms across), and the instrument still resolves sub-nanometer height changes via cantilever deflection.*

## The boundary (what this does not cover)

Specialized CO-functionalized tips in ultrahigh-vacuum non-contact AFM can approach single-molecule apex sharpness. That is a different instrument than the one in the reel, and the reel does not describe it.

## Why this belongs in the Foundry

This is the claim-boundary discipline applied to public material: state the claim, state exactly what would kill it, check it against independent sources, publish the verdict with the boundary attached. The reel had a hook but no boundary. The Foundry is the boundary.
