## Ranking diagnosis

Rank 1 and rank 2 were effectively tied under the non-predictive sharing-risk score:

- rank 1 score: `-0.0013322869724495961`
- rank 2 score: `-0.001412172856304597`
- absolute difference: about `0.0000799`

That tiny difference came from the measured OLG popularity-overlap proxy, not from a validated draw-probability model. In other words, the system acted as though a very small prize-sharing heuristic difference justified a deterministic ordering even though the score had no demonstrated relationship to which line would be drawn.

The strongest historical predictive model before the draw was `recency / 26`, but its corrected p-value was `1.0`, so the predictive gate was false. Its frozen candidate was:

- predictive candidate: `02 04 10 16 20 33 45` → **1 main hit** (`4`)

This draw therefore gives no reason to promote the historical prediction layer.

## Exploratory family statistic

For the fixed pre-draw top-10 family, the observed maximum was four hits. A deterministic Monte Carlo null that keeps the ten frozen lines exactly as they were and randomizes only a valid 7-of-52 draw gives approximately a **3.55%** chance of seeing at least one four-hit-or-better line in that family (500,000 simulations, seed `20260819`).

This number is **exploratory only**. The family-maximum metric was not predeclared before the draw, so it is not a valid prospective significance claim and must not be used to retune the model on this same result.

## Decision for Selection V4

Do **not** tune weights toward `4,13,21,26,39,43,48` and do not reward rank 2 after the fact.

Instead:

1. Keep candidate generation and predictive diagnostics frozen for the next prospective block.
2. Add a formal post-draw evaluator for every frozen rank, the played line, and the model candidate.
3. Treat tiny differences among non-predictive sharing-risk scores as an equivalence band rather than evidence that rank 1 is more drawable than rank 2.
4. Predeclare family-level metrics before the next draw/block: rank-1 hits, best-of-top-5 hits, best-of-top-10 hits, and baseline comparisons.
5. Compare against pure random/Quick Pick and a shape-matched random baseline over many future draws before claiming any accuracy edge.
6. Do not allow a predictive candidate to influence a played selection until the corrected prospective gate passes on unseen draws.

## Claim boundary

One draw is not enough to establish a lottery-prediction edge. The useful result here is narrower: the pre-draw Forge family contained a four-hit line at rank 2 while the played rank-1 line had two hits. That is evidence that the current deterministic ranking overstates the meaning of tiny sharing-risk score differences; it is not evidence that Forge can predict a fair random draw.
